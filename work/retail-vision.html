Problem

Checkout is the slowest part of grocery shopping. Self-checkout just moves the work to the shopper.

Solution

Scan items on your phone as you shop; in-store cameras verify; associates resolve the rare mismatch.

Status

39 screens across 3 roles designed. The computer-vision pipeline and live inventory sync are in progress.

Design principle
Cameras never add or charge products.

Only what the shopper scans gets charged. Cameras only watch shelf interactions. When the two disagree, the system asks an associate for a quick check. It never accuses the shopper.

02 — Key decisions

Designing for the edge cases first.

01

Failure states are first-class

Barcode not recognized, payment failed, produce by weight: each has its own screen, because that's where trust is won or lost.

02

Evidence over alerts

Every flag shows a 12-second clip, an event timeline and a reconciliation summary. "Incorrect detection" is a primary action, not a hidden one.

03

A real-time cart

Totals and stock levels update as items are added or removed. Shoppers always see the number they'll pay.

04

Run it at the edge

An edge console and camera-health views let managers see coverage gaps (47 of 48 online) before they turn into missed events.

03 — What's next

From Figma to the aisle.

Now I'm building the computer-vision pipeline that identifies products from in-store cameras, and the inventory integration that keeps carts and stock in sync in real time.
